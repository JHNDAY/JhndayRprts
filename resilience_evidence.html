Evidence of Progress in Challenging Times

Last 6 Months: February – July 2026

While headlines focus on institutional threats, government-funded systems and democratic institutions continue advancing science, protecting rights, expanding education, and safeguarding the environment. Here's the evidence.

⚕️ Health Research: Breakthroughs from NIH-Funded Labs

June 2026

Gene Editing Therapy Shows 87% Success Rate

A Phase 3 clinical trial of gene editing therapy showed an 87% reduction in attack rates relative to placebo, with 60% of patients remaining attack-free entirely over 6 months. This marks the first successful in vivo gene editing treatment for late-stage disease in human patients.

87% reduction in attack rates • 60% remained attack-free
Source
Works in Progress, June 2026
June 2026

Portable TB Diagnostic Test Approved

NIH researchers developed and validated a portable diagnostic test for tuberculosis that produces rapid and accurate results. The test makes TB detection accessible in remote and resource-limited settings, potentially saving millions of lives globally.

Rapid • Portable • Accurate results in field settings
Source
NIH Research Matters
May 2026

CRISPR Treatments Moving to Clinical Care

CRISPR cures for sickle cell disease and gene therapy for hemophilia A have moved from experimental protocols to standard clinical treatment options. These represent a shift from one-size-fits-all care to personalized, potentially curative medicine.

Sickle cell disease & hemophilia A: Now in clinical treatment
Source
Medscape, May 2026
June 2026

Blood Test Predicts Alzheimer's Onset

NIH-funded researchers created a blood test that predicts the start of Alzheimer's disease symptoms with high accuracy. Early detection enables intervention strategies that can slow or delay cognitive decline.

Early detection enables preventive intervention
Source
NIH Research Matters

⚖️ Democratic Institutions: Court Victories Protecting Rights

June 2026

Court Blocks Executive Order on Mail-In Voting

A federal court ruled that nonpartisan voting rights groups could proceed with their challenge to an executive order restricting mail-in voting. The court recognized that the order attempts to override state election laws and threatens voting rights.

Voting rights groups: Challenge to proceed to preliminary injunction hearing
Source
ACLU, June 2026
June 2026

Maryland Court Dismisses DOJ Voter Data Lawsuit

A federal court dismissed the Department of Justice's attempt to compel Maryland to provide complete voter registration data. The court protected voter privacy against what it found to be an unlawful fishing expedition.

Voter privacy protected • DOJ lawsuit dismissed
Source
Democracy Docket
June 2026

Federal Court Blocks Unlawful SAVE Database Expansion

A D.C. federal court ruled that the Trump administration's unlawful expansion of the SAVE system to include sensitive records on all Americans violated voting rights and privacy protections. The court rejected the government's attempt to stay the order.

SAVE database expansion blocked • Privacy protections upheld
Source
Democracy Forward, July 2026
June 2026

Multiple State Voter Data Victories

Federal courts rejected DOJ attempts to seize voter registration databases in Michigan and other states. Courts recognized these as attempts to weaponize voter data and upheld states' authority to protect voter privacy while administering elections.

Michigan, Virginia, Louisiana: Voter data protected across multiple states
Source
Democracy Docket

📚 Education: Expanding Access & Opportunity

2026

California Students Show Gains in Core Subjects

Results from 2024-25 statewide assessments show California students made meaningful gains across English language arts, mathematics, and science. All grades in all subjects saw increases, demonstrating systemic improvement in public K-12 education.

All grades, all subjects: Measurable improvement statewide
Source
California Budget Summary, 2026
July 2026

Workforce Pell Expands Aid to Short-Term Programs

Workforce Pell Grants launched in July 2026, allowing Pell Grants to support accredited programs shorter than 15 weeks. This opens federal financial aid to job-training credentials previously inaccessible to low-income students, reducing barriers to employment.

Short-term, high-value credentials now federally supported
Source
EAB Analysis, 2026
2026

Community College Confidence Reaches 56%

Public confidence in community colleges has reached 56%, up significantly from previous years. This far exceeds confidence in four-year institutions (44%), positioning community colleges as trusted pathways to affordable education and workforce readiness.

56% public confidence in two-year colleges (vs 44% four-year)
Source
EAB, January 2026

K-12 and community college partnerships continue expanding dual enrollment programs. Teacher credentialing initiatives (like California's effort to credential 115 high school teachers) enable more students to earn college credit before graduation, accelerating postsecondary attainment.

K-12 to college pathways strengthening; equity focus expanding

Q1 2026

91 EPA Enforcement Actions in Q1

The EPA finalized 91 settlement agreements in the first quarter of 2026 with companies across the United States for environmental violations, resulting in $3.4M in fines. Actions targeted Clean Air Act, hazardous waste, and pesticide violations.

91 settlements • $3.4M in penalties • CAA, RCRA, FIFRA violations

Q2 2026

109 EPA Settlement Agreements in Q2

In the second quarter of 2026, EPA enforcement actions increased to 109 settlement agreements, with $7.1M in penalties. Focus included Clean Water Act violations, lead-based paint hazards, hazardous waste mismanagement, and emergency release notifications.

109 settlements • $7.1M in penalties • Water, waste, lead violations

In Q1 2026, the largest EPA fine was $781,175 against a building materials manufacturer for Clean Air Act violations and hazardous air pollutant emissions. The company was required to modify its operating permit and submit an emissions reduction plan.
